About Mahabodhi Temple

The Mahabodhi Temple in Bodh Gaya is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the best places to visit in Bihar. It marks the place where Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree.

The temple complex includes the main Mahabodhi Temple, the Bodhi tree, and several other sacred sites. The current temple was built in the 5th-6th century AD during the Gupta period, though the site has been a pilgrimage destination for over 2,000 years.

Historical Significance

Mahabodhi Temple holds immense religious and historical significance. It is believed that Prince Siddhartha attained enlightenment and became the Buddha here around 528 BCE. The temple has been destroyed and rebuilt several times throughout history, with the current structure dating back to the Gupta period.

Architecture

The temple features classic Indian architecture with intricate carvings and sculptures. The main tower stands 55 meters tall and is surrounded by four smaller towers. The temple walls are adorned with scenes from Buddha's life and various Buddhist motifs.

Visitor Information

The temple is open to visitors of all faiths. The best time to visit is during the winter months (October to March) when the weather is pleasant. The temple hosts various Buddhist festivals throughout the year, with Buddha Purnima being the most significant.
