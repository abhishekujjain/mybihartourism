Barabar Caves

The oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India dating back to the Mauryan period

About Barabar Caves

Barabar Caves in Jehanabad is a sought after tourist destination. These ancient rock-cut caves date back to the Mauryan period (3rd century BC) and are the oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India.

The caves were commissioned by Emperor Ashoka for the Ajivika sect, a non-Buddhist religious group. The remarkable feature of these caves is their highly polished interior surfaces, which still shine after 2,300 years.

Historical Significance

Barabar Caves are of immense historical importance as they represent the earliest example of rock-cut architecture in India. The caves have been mentioned in various ancient texts and have been studied by archaeologists for their unique construction techniques.

Architecture

The caves consist of four main chambers: Lomas Rishi, Sudama, Vishvakarma, and Karan Chaupar. The Lomas Rishi cave is particularly famous for its beautifully carved entrance resembling a wooden hut. The interior surfaces are incredibly smooth, showcasing the advanced craftsmanship of the Mauryan period.

Visitor Information

The caves are open to visitors and offer a unique glimpse into ancient Indian rock-cut architecture. The site is well-maintained and provides information about the historical significance of each cave. The best time to visit is from October to March.
